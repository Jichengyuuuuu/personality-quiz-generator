# Entertainment divination quiz guide

Use this guide for fortune-themed, oracle-style, intuition-choice, and psychological-projection quizzes. Build an evocative entertainment experience without presenting invented predictions as facts.

## 1. Keep the format inside the skill's scope

Use this mode when participant choices lead to a symbolic result such as:

- a current energy or emotional weather;
- a type of opportunity worth noticing;
- an inner need, relationship pattern, or growth reminder;
- a symbolic guide, object, animal, place, or element;
- a near-term theme expressed as possibility rather than fate.

Use another skill or tool for natal charts, birth-time calculations, traditional divination systems, or genuinely random card draws that require specialist rules. Do not imply that this quiz performs those systems.

## 2. Convert predictions into reflective promises

Write a promise that feels mysterious but remains honest.

Prefer:

- Which kind of opportunity are you most ready to notice?
- Choose a moonlit path and reveal the energy guiding your next step.
- What does your intuition want you to make room for right now?

Avoid:

- Exactly what will happen to you next month?
- When will you become rich?
- Which illness are you destined to develop?

Transform certainty into attention, possibility, readiness, or reflection. Replace “will happen” with “may be worth noticing,” “you may be ready for,” or “your choices point toward.”

## 3. Design a hidden reflective model

Keep the ordinary deterministic quiz model underneath the mystical presentation. Select dimensions that fit the promise, such as:

- seek novelty ↔ seek stability;
- act outwardly ↔ process inwardly;
- trust intuition ↔ verify through evidence;
- connect with others ↔ restore independently;
- release control ↔ create structure.

Map every answer to stable dimension scores. Treat symbols as a presentation layer over these patterns, not as supernatural evidence.

## 4. Write intuitive but meaningful questions

Mix two item types so the result is not based on arbitrary decoration alone:

1. **Symbolic choices**: a door, landscape, light, object, sound, color, or imagined destination.
2. **Behavioral choices**: what the participant notices, protects, avoids, reaches for, or does next in a concrete situation.

Let symbolic choices create atmosphere and behavioral choices create interpretability. Keep every option appealing, visually distinct, and free from obvious lucky or unlucky rankings.

Do not ask users to disclose trauma, diagnoses, pregnancy status, financial distress, or other sensitive information merely to create drama.

## 5. Write the result as a symbolic reading

Give every result:

- a memorable symbol and result name;
- one-line reading expressed as a current tendency or invitation;
- answer-linked evidence using selected images or behaviors;
- a strength and its trade-off;
- one low-stakes reflection prompt or action;
- a share line that works without claiming certainty;
- a visible entertainment disclaimer.

Good result language:

> Your choices lean toward quiet preparation. The “Tide Lantern” suggests making space before committing to the next visible move.

Weak result language:

> The universe guarantees that a wealthy stranger will change your life within 30 days.

## 6. Preserve deterministic scoring

Use the same scoring and tie-break rules as any other quiz. The same answers must produce the same result. Do not secretly randomize outcomes or use a final “pick a card” question to override the accumulated scores.

If the user explicitly requests a random draw, state that randomness is a separate entertainment mechanic. Do not describe it as evidence or mix it invisibly into a deterministic personality result.

## 7. Apply safety boundaries

Never produce:

- threats, curses, fear-based countdowns, or coercive sharing;
- guaranteed romance, wealth, career, pregnancy, health, legal, or mortality claims;
- instructions to replace professional advice or stop treatment;
- invented rarity or accuracy percentages;
- claims that a symbol, card, or answer proves an external fact.

Use a concise participant-facing disclosure such as:

> 本测试仅供娱乐与自我观察，结果是对选择倾向的象征性解读，不构成现实预测或专业建议。

Match the disclosure language to the participant locale.
